## The headline finding

Across the studies we have run for suppliers over the last two years, the pattern is consistent: research that educators found genuinely useful produced better commercial outcomes than research designed to produce a marketing asset.

That sounds like a platitude. It isn't. The two look almost identical on a project plan and behave completely differently.

## Why it matters

An organisation commissioning research to support a campaign tends to write the questions around what it hopes to prove. Educators can tell. Response rates fall, the findings are thin, and the resulting report gets read by nobody outside the company that paid for it.

An organisation commissioning research to find something out asks better questions, gets fuller answers, and ends up with something educators pass to each other.

## Key findings

- Studies framed around an educator problem recruited participants substantially faster than studies framed around a product category.
- Qualitative depth mattered more than sample size for the findings that changed clients' plans.
- The engagement value of a study was concentrated in what happened *after* publication — briefings, events, follow-up content — not in the report itself.

## What we'd do with it

Start with a question educators want answered. Publish what you find, including the parts that are inconvenient. Then build the briefing, the event and the opted-in audience around it.

The pipeline is real, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. It just isn't the starting point.
