There is a particular conversation we have several times a year. An organisation has bought, built or inherited a substantial database of schools. They are pleased with it, and reasonably so. Then they ask us to help them market to it.

The database is not the problem. The assumption underneath it is.

## Knowing who exists is not knowing who matters

A list of 24,000 schools is a list of 24,000 organisations that exist. It is not a list of organisations with your problem, your budget cycle, your kind of leadership, or any current reason to care.

Send the same message to all of them and you will get a response rate that looks like a rounding error, then conclude that email doesn't work in education.

## The uncomfortable arithmetic

Sending the wrong message to more schools doesn't make it more right. It makes it more wrong, more often, to more people who now recognise your name and associate it with something irrelevant.

Reach is cheap. Relevance is not. The organisations that do well in education are usually the ones that decided to talk to fewer schools about something those schools were actually dealing with.

## So what is a market?

A market is the subset of that list where a need genuinely exists, where you can articulate that need better than they currently can, and where you have earned enough attention to be listened to.

That is a much smaller number. It is also a much better one.
